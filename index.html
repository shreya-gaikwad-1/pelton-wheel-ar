The Pelton wheel

A water jet hits 18 split buckets and spins a shaft. Turn the model, watch it run, or place it on your own table.

Drag to rotate, pinch or scroll to zoom. On a phone, tap "View in your room" and point the camera at a table or floor.

What you are looking at

Nozzle

Turns the water's pressure into a fast, narrow jet. A needle inside it adjusts the flow.

Water jet

Leaves the nozzle at a speed set by the head: v = √(2gH).

Buckets

Each bucket has two cups with a splitter ridge in the middle. It divides the jet and turns it almost 180°, which pushes the wheel.

Disc and rim

Holds the 18 buckets evenly around the circle.

Hub and shaft

Carry the torque to a generator or machine.

Bearings and frame

Support the shaft and keep the wheel aligned with the jet.

How it works

All of the water's pressure energy becomes speed in the nozzle. The jet then strikes the buckets at atmospheric pressure, so the wheel is pushed by the change in the water's momentum, not by pressure. The water leaves the bucket slowly and falls away into the tailrace.

Jet speed: v = √(2 g H)

Power: P = η ρ g Q H

H is the head in metres, Q the flow in m³/s, ρ the water density (1000 kg/m³) and η the overall efficiency.
